Fast food calories: the chains’ figures, and how to manage

A fast food meal is not a slip you cannot make up for, as long as you know what it contains. The big chains publish their nutritional values; what really tips the balance is the sides, the drinks and the size of the meal.

In short

A classic burger and medium fries come to 850 to 950 kcal at McDonald’s or Burger King, and nearly 1,900 kcal at Five Guys (values published in France). The fries often weigh as much as the burger. To lighten things without giving up: a small burger, nuggets or a salad on the side, and a sugar-free drink.

Fast food meal calories: the figures

These are the values the chains publish in France for the whole product, recorded from their official websites in October 2026. Recipes and portions vary from country to country:

  • Big Mac + medium fries (McDonald’s): 530 + 328 = 858 kcal;
  • Whopper + medium fries (Burger King): 629 + 309 = 938 kcal;
  • Cheeseburger + medium fries (Five Guys): 801 + 1,073 = 1,874 kcal;
  • Cheeseburger + 4 Chicken McNuggets (McDonald’s): 300 + 174 = 474 kcal.

Recipes change: check the chain’s nutrition information if in doubt.

What pushes fast food calories up

The burger is often only half the meal. Medium fries provide around 300 kcal at the classic chains, and over 1,000 kcal at Five Guys, where the portion is much larger. Then come sauces, desserts and sugary drinks. Going “large” mainly increases the fries and the drink.

And estimating by eye is misleading: in a study in American fast food restaurants, customers underestimated the calorie content of their meal, all the more so as the meal got bigger [1].

Eating fast food on a diet: how to build your meal

  • Pick a simple burger: a cheeseburger or hamburger rather than a double or a “steakhouse” burger (829 kcal at Burger King France).
  • Swap or share the fries: nuggets, a salad, or a small portion.
  • Drink sugar-free: water, diet soda, coffee.
  • Adjust the rest of the day without skipping it: a lighter evening meal is often enough. It is the weekly average that counts (calorie deficit).
  • Keep some protein: a meal high in fat and low in protein is not very filling (protein per day).

Are the stated calories reliable?

A study measured in the lab the calories of dishes from American restaurants and fast food chains: on average, the gap with the stated values was small, but nearly one dish in five exceeded the stated value by at least 100 kcal [2]. For a standardised chain product, the published value remains the best reference available. For a dish in an ordinary restaurant, there is no official value: you can only estimate.

What we don’t know

Published values assume a standard recipe; the amount of sauce or fries served varies from one restaurant to another. The chains do not publish the weight of their products, which makes it impossible to check their figures per 100 g. For typical dishes, the portion size is an estimate, not a measurement.
