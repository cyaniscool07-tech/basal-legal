The calculation in detail

expenditure = average intake − (end weight − starting weight) × 7,700 ÷ days

One kilogram of body mass represents about 7,700 kcal. If you lost weight, you spent more than you ate: the difference, spread per day, is added to your intake.

Why it is more accurate than a formula

A formula like Mifflin-St Jeor gives the average expenditure of people who resemble you on four criteria, with a typical error of about 10%. Your weight, on the other hand, reacts to what you actually spend. Over a few weeks, it tells you whether your intake was above or below your expenditure, and by how much.

Measuring well

  • Weigh yourself every morning, under the same conditions, and average by week: a single day’s weight moves by several hundred grams with water and salt.
  • Log everything you eat, including the off days. Forgotten meals make your expenditure look lower than it is.
  • Count at least three to four weeks. Below 10 days, the tool refuses to calculate: noise outweighs the signal.

What this calculation does not know

The start of a diet skews the calculation. In the first days the body mostly loses water and glycogen, far lighter in calories than fat. Measuring from the second week avoids overestimating your expenditure.

It is only as good as your log. A steady 200 kcal error in your meals ends up as is in the result.

It is not medical advice. An expenditure far from what you would expect can have a cause worth showing a doctor.
