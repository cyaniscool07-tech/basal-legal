Beef mince calories: 5, 10, 15 or 20% fat, raw or cooked

Beef mince at 5% fat provides 130 kcal per 100 g raw; at 20% fat it provides 249: almost double, for slightly less protein. The percentage on the pack matters more than anything else, and the value changes again depending on whether you weigh before or after cooking.

In short

Per 100 g of raw beef mince: 130 kcal at 5% fat, 170 kcal at 10%, 215 kcal at 15% and 249 kcal at 20%. Cooked, the same meat provides 155, 210, 239 and 265 kcal per 100 g, because it has lost water. A 100 g raw patty at 5% fat provides about 22 g of protein.

Carbohydrates are zero or close to it. The whole difference in calories comes from fat: 4.6 g per 100 g raw at 5%, 20 g at 20%.

For a 100 g or 125 g patty

Shop-bought beef patties usually weigh 100 g or 125 g raw (the weight is on the pack):

  • 100 g, 5%: 130 kcal and 22 g of protein;
  • 100 g, 15%: 215 kcal and 20 g of protein;
  • 125 g, 5%: 163 kcal and 27 g of protein;
  • 125 g, 15%: 269 kcal and 25 g of protein.

Between a 125 g patty at 5% and the same at 15%, the gap is about 106 kcal. One patty a day for a week adds up to a little over 700 kcal.

Why cooked mince has more calories per 100 g

Cooking does not create calories: the meat loses water, so what is left is more concentrated. Based on the Ciqual values, and assuming the protein stays in the meat, a 100 g raw patty at 5% fat weighs only about 86 g once cooked. Those 86 g at 155 kcal per 100 g come to 133 kcal: almost exactly the 130 kcal of the raw patty.

What matters is choosing the line that matches what you weighed. Weighed on its pack: raw value. Weighed on the plate: cooked value. The details, for every food, are in the guide weighing food raw or cooked.

5% or 15% mince: which to choose

  • For a cut: 5% gives more protein for fewer calories. It is the simplest choice for sticking to a calorie deficit.
  • For taste and texture: 15% stays juicier. It is not a bad choice, as long as you log it as such: the most common mistake is eating 15% and logging 5%.
  • For protein: from 5 to 15%, it stays close to 20 g per 100 g raw. At 20%, it drops to 17 g.

To place these amounts in your day, the guide how much protein per day gives the benchmarks, and the protein calculator works it out for your weight.

What we don’t know

Ciqual values are averages. A given brand can differ by a few calories; its label is the reference. Water loss varies with cooking: well-done meat loses more than rare, and the cooked weight worked out above (86 g) is an estimate, not a measurement. Some fat melts into the pan; Ciqual does not say how much, and it is not deducted here.
