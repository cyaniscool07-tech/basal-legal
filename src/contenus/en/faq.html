Frequently asked questions

What Basal does, what it does not do, and what stays free. The answers describe version 1.4 of the app. Another question: contact@basal-app.fr.

Is Basal really free?

Yes, for the most part, with no time limit and no ads: meal and macro tracking, barcode scanner, weight and trend, adaptive expenditure, strength sessions, recipes, the Community and step sync.

An optional Premium subscription adds eight features: the weekly review, volume per muscle group, protein on training and rest days, the advanced metabolism layer, advanced strategies, and three meal assistants (organise, prepare and optimise the week). The review and each assistant can be tried once for free.

Which devices does Basal work on?

On iPhone (App Store) and Android (Google Play). There is no version designed for iPad and no web version. The app is in English, French, Spanish and German, following the phone’s language.

Do I need to create an account?

No. The whole app works without an account, without an email and without signing up: your data stays on your phone.

An optional account is for getting your data back on another phone (it is then synced to a server hosted in Ireland) and for posting in the Community. The details are in a calorie app without an account.

Can Basal create a strength programme for me?

Not automatically: Basal does not generate programmes. You can build your routines yourself, exercise by exercise, or pick up routines and programmes shared in the Community, sorted by level.

To get started, the guide how many sets per muscle per week gives the orders of magnitude.

Does Basal work with a smartwatch?

There is no Apple Watch app, and no direct connection to Garmin, Fitbit or Samsung. Basal does, however, read your step count from Apple Health (iPhone) or Health Connect (Android), for free: if your watch sends its steps there, Basal picks them up. It reads and writes nothing else.

Can I add a meal by taking a photo of it?

No, Basal does not recognise dishes from photos: these estimates often get the quantities wrong, and quantities make up most of the calories. The camera is for reading the barcode of packaged products, live or from a sharp photo when live reading fails.

For a home-made dish, food search and the calculated recipes give more reliable values.

Is the food database reliable?

Basal includes ANSES’s Ciqual 2025 table, over 3,300 foods analysed and published by the French health agency. Scanned products come from Open Food Facts, a collaborative database: their values are those on the labels, entered by contributors, so sometimes incomplete.

On top of that come the menus of six fast-food chains and an estimate per type of restaurant dish.

How is my calorie target calculated?

Basal starts from the Mifflin-St Jeor formula (age, sex, height, weight), multiplies it by your activity level, then applies your goal. After that, it compares what you eat with how your weight actually changes to correct this estimate, in small steps.

All the formulas and their limits are on the Method page, and you can try them with the expenditure calculator.

What is the estimated 1RM?

It is the maximum load you could lift just once, estimated from a set of several reps, without having to attempt it. Basal uses the Epley formula: load × (1 + reps ÷ 30). It is used to track your progress from one session to the next.

The 1RM calculator does the same calculation and gives the loads by percentage.

Can I add my own exercises?

Yes. The library has 873 exercises, and you can create your own from the exercise selection screen: they are then available in your routines like the others.

Is my data backed up?

It is stored on your phone. So as not to lose it when you change device, you have two options:

  • create an account, which syncs your data automatically;
  • create a backup file (Settings, then My data), to restore on the new phone.

Exporting as JSON or CSV is for reading your data elsewhere: it cannot be imported back. The details are in the privacy policy.

Does the app work without the internet?

Yes, for the most part: tracking, weight, sessions, recipes and searching the Ciqual foods work offline. You need a connection to read a barcode, search Open Food Facts, use the Community, sync an account or subscribe to Premium.

Does Basal sell my data?

No. Basal does not sell, rent or share any data for advertising purposes, and the app contains no ads and no ad trackers. The only third-party services are there to make it work (sync, subscription, crash detection) and are listed in the privacy policy.
