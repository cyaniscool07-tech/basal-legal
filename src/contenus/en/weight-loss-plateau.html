Losing or gaining weight

Weight loss plateau: why it stalls, and what to do

After a few weeks or months, weight loss almost always slows down. It is not necessarily a failure, nor a “stuck” metabolism: it is partly arithmetic, partly a food log that drifts. Before cutting further, you need to know which of the two you are facing.

In short

First check that it is a real plateau: at least two weeks with no drop in the trend, with a complete log. Then recalculate your expenditure at your current weight. Then choose: adjust slightly, take a break at maintenance, or accept a phase of stabilisation.

Plateau or normal fluctuation: check first

The scale also weighs water, salt and gut content: a gap of 1 to 2 kg from one day to the next is common (daily weight fluctuations). One week without a drop proves nothing. It is a plateau when the trend, the smoothed average of your weigh-ins, has stopped falling for at least two weeks while the log is kept every day.

In women, the menstrual cycle can mask a loss for one to two weeks through water retention: comparing equivalent weeks from one cycle to the next avoids jumping to conclusions.

Weight loss slowing down: why it is normal

Loss slows first for a mechanical reason: a lighter body burns less, at rest and on the move. The same intake that created a deficit at the start creates a smaller one a few kilos later. Models that track these changes show that the “7,700 kcal = 1 kg” rule overestimates long-term loss, because it ignores this drop in expenditure [1].

On top of that comes an adaptation: expenditure falls a little more than weight loss explains, what is called adaptive metabolism [2]. It exists, but on its own it is not enough to explain a complete stop in weight loss.

A drifting food log: the most common cause

In a now-classic study, people who said they could not lose weight despite dieting underestimated their intake by 47% on average and overestimated their activity by 51% [3]. Nobody is lying: cooking oil, snacks and weekend meals get forgotten, and people weigh less as the habit settles in.

A mathematical model of weight loss also shows that the plateau seen around six months in studies is better explained by a gradual loosening of the diet than by metabolism alone [4]. Before changing anything, three days of strictly weighing everything you eat tell you a lot.

Weight loss plateau: what to do

Once the log is checked, four options remain, from the gentlest to the most decisive:

  1. Recalculate your expenditure at your current weight: the goal set three months ago no longer matches your body today (work out your real expenditure).
  2. Adjust slightly, by around 5 to 10% of expenditure, without going below your basal metabolic rate.
  3. Take a break of one to two weeks at maintenance (diet break). In a trial in men with overweight, alternating two weeks of dieting with two weeks at energy balance led to more fat loss than continuous dieting over the same total dieting time [5].
  4. Accept a stabilisation phase, or even come off the diet with a reverse diet if you are close to your goal.

What we don’t know

There is no way to measure, in a given person, how much of a plateau comes from adapted expenditure and how much from the log. Studies on diet breaks are still few and involve specific profiles. The app’s thresholds (eight and sixteen weeks, 14 days) are cautious choices, not proven values.
