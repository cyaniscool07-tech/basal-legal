These are starting points, not a prescription. The calculation rests on an estimate of expenditure that validation studies commonly report as about 10% off — that is 250 kcal a day on an expenditure of 2,500. Your macros inherit that uncertainty. What corrects them is not a finer formula, it is watching your own data over several weeks.

Why not a simple percentage

The most common method splits calories into fixed shares — for example 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat. It has a flaw that only shows in the cases where it matters most: it makes your needs depend on your calorie target, when they depend on your body.

On a low target, 30% of 1,100 kcal gives about 82 g of protein and 37 g of fat. Both are too low, and they become too low precisely when they matter most — during weight loss, when the aim is to keep muscle and hormonal balance.

Hence the order used here: protein and fat are set first, from body weight. Carbs take what is left.

The calculation in detail

Protein

In grams per kilogram, depending on the goal, with a top-up from three sessions a week — the point at which training creates a real extra need.

  • Loss — 1.8 g/kg, or 2.2 g/kg if you train.
  • Maintenance — 1.4 g/kg, or 1.8 g/kg if you train.
  • Muscle gain — 1.6 g/kg, or 2.0 g/kg if you train.

These values sit within the range the resistance-training literature commonly reports, 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg, with returns that flatten beyond it.

Reference weight, and why it is not always your weight

Multiplying by total weight becomes a problem above a certain build: excess fat tissue does not have the same protein needs as the rest of the body. The calculation therefore uses your actual weight up to a BMI of 25, then counts only a quarter of the excess above it.

That is the factor of the clinical adjusted-weight formula, used exactly where total weight would distort a prescription. The threshold sits at the top of the so-called normal range, not in the middle: a low threshold would trim protein for people who have nothing to trim. In practice, a person of ordinary build sees no difference.

Fat

The larger of two values is used: 0.8 g per kilogram of reference weight, or 30% of calories. The first is a floor that does not depend on the calorie total — below it, the risk of essential fatty acid deficiency and hormonal disruption is documented, whatever the goal.

Carbs and fibre

Carbs take what remains once protein and fat are covered, without ever going below zero: in an extreme case — very high weight and very low target — the priority goes to the safety of the other two rather than to the exact total. Fibre follows the standard recommendation of 14 g per 1,000 kcal.

The calorie floor

The calorie target never goes below your basal metabolic rate, nor below 800 kcal. It is not an arbitrary minimum: it is an individual physiological threshold, calculated from your sex, age, weight and height. If the pace you chose would take you lower, the calculator tells you instead of quietly correcting it.
