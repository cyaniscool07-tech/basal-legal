The calculation in detail

1RM ≈ load × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

Example: 80 kg lifted 8 times
80 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30)101.3 kg
100 kg lifted once: already a real maximum, kept as is100 kg

The Epley formula (1985) is the most widely used. At one rep it would return 1.033 times the load, although the load lifted is already the maximum: the app and this calculator therefore keep it as is, rather than inflating a real record by 3%.

What it is for

An estimated 1RM lets you compare sessions with different rep counts: 80 kg for 8 and 90 kg for 4 give almost the same 1RM (101.3 and 102 kg). That is how the app tracks an exercise’s progress from one week to the next.

What this calculation does not know

It is an estimate. The link between reps and maximum load varies with the exercise, your training and the day’s fatigue.

It is not an invitation to test your max. A one-rep attempt is done warmed up, with controlled technique, and with a spotter or safeties on exercises that need them.
