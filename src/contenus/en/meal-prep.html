Meal prep: recipes and planning to prepare your week

Cook once, eat for several days. The principle of meal prep (or batch cooking) is simple; what makes it succeed or fail are three choices: the recipes, the storage calendar, and what you freeze from the start.

Choosing recipes that keep

Not all recipes age the same way. Those best suited to cooking ahead have three things in common: they reheat without spoiling (stews, curries, sauces, pulses), they portion easily into boxes, and their ingredients do not go watery after two days. Fried food, dressed leafy salads and overcooked pasta, on the other hand, do not take the wait well.

Nutrition-wise, a weekly prep is also a chance to lock in your protein: by choosing a rich base (chicken, lentils, fish, eggs), each box brings a known serving, instead of a day-to-day guess.

How long it keeps

These are indicative guidelines from health authorities, at 4 °C at most, for dishes chilled quickly — never a guarantee:

  • Home-cooked dishes (meat, poultry, fish, vegetables, cooked eggs): 3 days in the fridge (ANSES, Santé publique France).
  • Soups: 2 days (Health Canada, lower bound).
  • Dishes with cooked rice: 24 hours. Rice can carry spores of Bacillus cereus, which survive cooking (Food Standards Scotland; ANSES describes the same risk).
  • Raw-egg preparations (chocolate mousse): 24 hours, do not freeze.
  • In the freezer, a home-made dish keeps its quality for 2 to 3 months (Health Canada); a soup, about 4 months.

Four rules apply to everything made ahead:

  • put it in the fridge within 2 hours of cooking;
  • a fridge at 4 °C at most, dishes stored in the coldest zone;
  • reheat all the way through (70 °C), until steam comes out;
  • thaw in the fridge, never at room temperature, and never refreeze.

Organising the week

The practical consequence of the times above: one Sunday session does not cover seven days in the fridge. Two ways to go about it, to combine:

  • Two short sessions, for example Sunday and Wednesday, each for three days.
  • Freeze on cooking day the portions planned beyond three days, and move them to the fridge the day before the meal.

Eat first what keeps least (rice, soups), keep what holds best for the end of the period, and write the cooking date on each box.

To count a serving accurately, weigh the whole dish once cooked and divide: details in the guide weighing raw or cooked.
