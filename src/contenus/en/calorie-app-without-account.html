Choosing an app

A calorie app without an account, that works offline

Counting calories without giving your email, without signing up and without a permanent connection is possible. Here is what that changes, what really works offline, how not to lose your data, and how to check that an app keeps its promises.

In short

Basal installs and works entirely without an account, without an email and without signing up, for free and with no ads. Tracking meals, weight and sessions works offline. You only need the internet to read a barcode, search Open Food Facts, the Community and the Premium subscription. To lose nothing when you change phone, a backup file is enough.

Why so many apps ask for an account

An account has real uses: getting your data back on another phone, sharing a subscription between devices. But it also serves other purposes: sending marketing emails, linking your usage to your identity, and making it harder to leave for another app.

To track what you eat, none of that is needed. A calorie counter can calculate everything on the phone: the food table, expenditure, targets and history fit in the device’s memory.

What Basal does without an account

Everything, except two things. Without creating an account, without an email and without signing up, you get:

  • meal, calorie and macro tracking, with ANSES’s Ciqual table (over 3,300 foods);
  • the barcode scanner;
  • weight, its trend and the expenditure recalculated from your data (adaptive metabolism);
  • strength sessions, routines and estimated 1RM;
  • calculated recipes, meal planning and the shopping list;
  • steps, read from Apple Health or Health Connect;
  • the Premium subscription, which can also be bought without an account.

The account, which is optional, is only for two things: syncing your data so you can get it back on another phone, and posting in the Community.

In practice, at the gym with no signal or when travelling, you can log everything. A packaged product can be caught up later, once connected, or added by hand with the values on the label.

Not losing your data

That is the trade-off: without an account, your data lives only on your phone. If you lose it or change it, the data does not follow you on its own. Two solutions:

  • A backup file: in Settings, then My data, “Create a backup” produces a file you keep wherever you like, and “Restore a backup” loads it onto the new phone. Still without an account.
  • An account, if you would rather sync happens on its own. It can be deleted at any time, with its data (account deletion).

Exporting as JSON or CSV is different: it is for reading your data in a spreadsheet or another app, and it cannot be imported back.

Checking an app yourself

“No account” and “offline” are easy to write on a store listing. Four checks take five minutes, on any app:

  1. The first launch. If a sign-up screen blocks access, or if “continue without an account” leads to a cut-down version, the promise does not hold.
  2. Airplane mode. Turn it on, then add an everyday food (an apple, rice). If the search returns nothing, the food database is not on the phone.
  3. The store listing. The App Store (“App Privacy”) and Google Play (“Data safety”) list the data collected and the data linked to your identity.
  4. The privacy policy. Look for the words “advertising”, “partners” and “sale”: an app with no ads has no reason to share your data with ad networks.

For Basal, the privacy policy lists every third-party service and its role.

What Basal does not do

No photo recognition of dishes: the camera reads barcodes, not plates.

No watch app: only steps come through, via Apple Health or Health Connect.

No iPad or web version: Basal is an app for iPhone and Android.
