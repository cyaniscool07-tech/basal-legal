Choosing an app

Comparison: MyFitnessPal, Yazio, Foodvisor and Basal

This page is written by the developer of Basal, so by someone with an interest in the answer. I wrote it anyway, trying to make it useful to someone who would not choose Basal: each app first gets what it does better than the others, and my own limits are listed like theirs.

Prices and features checked in October 2026, for France, in euros. These apps change their offers regularly, and prices differ from one country to another — if you read this page long afterwards, or outside France, check the stores directly before deciding.

What each one does best

MyFitnessPal

The largest food database on the market, built over more than fifteen years. Available in many languages, with a mature ecosystem of integrations. If you travel or scan products from all over, it is the one that will find the entry.

Yazio

A German product, very polished, with the most generous free version of the three — the barcode scanner is included. Good coverage of European supermarket products, and intermittent fasting far more developed than elsewhere.

Foodvisor

A French app, built around photo recognition: you photograph your plate and it estimates what is on it. It is the fastest logging of the lot when it works, and no one else has made it the core of their product.

The real question is not the price, it is what is free

All these apps are “free” to download. The difference lies elsewhere: in what you can really do without paying, and above all in what has moved behind the subscription over the years.

  • MyFitnessPal moved its barcode scanner into the paid offer. It was the most used feature of its free version, and its move to Premium is largely what fuelled the search for alternatives.
  • Foodvisor keeps what makes it interesting for subscribers: photo recognition, recipes, courses. The free version is essentially limited to manual logging.
  • Yazio is the most generous of the three: the scanner stays free, and the free version is enough for many people.
  • Basal keeps all of the tracking free: calories, macros, scanner, weight, activity, steps, unlimited strength training, recipes, meal planning, shopping list and export. The subscription adds the analyses and three meal assistants.

Prices

  • MyFitnessPal Premium — around €9.99 a month or €49.99 a year.
  • Yazio Pro — around €6.99 a month or €44.99 a year.
  • Foodvisor Premium — around €59.99 a year.
  • Basal Premium — €4.99 a month or €39.99 a year.

These amounts change, and the stores sometimes show first-year promotions that make comparisons misleading. The number that matters is the renewal price, not the first bill.

What none of the three does

MyFitnessPal, Yazio and Foodvisor are nutrition apps. They sometimes record sessions, as calories burned — but none of them keeps a real strength-training log, and none links the two diaries.

Three questions therefore go unanswered, whichever of the three you choose: how many sets each muscle group really gets over a week, what you eat on rest days compared with training days, and whether your lifts progress while your weight goes down. You need both diaries to answer them, and that is Basal’s reason for being. The details are on the nutrition and strength training page.

And what Basal does not do

For this page to keep its value, the end of the list is needed too. Basal exists only in English, French, Spanish and German, has no photo recognition of meals, has no news feed or messaging (its Community is only for sharing recipes, routines and programmes), and cannot import a history from another app. Its food database combines ANSES’s Ciqual table for everyday foods and Open Food Facts, an open, collaborative database, for packaged products: very complete for common products in France, sparser for rare items and for products sold elsewhere.

How to choose

If you do not do strength training

Yazio is probably the best features-to-price ratio of the four, and its free version is enough for many people.

If you hate logging your meals

Foodvisor, for the photo — provided you accept that most of it is behind the subscription.

If you scan products from all over the world

MyFitnessPal, for the size of its database, knowing that the scanner requires the subscription.

If you lift weights

Basal, because it is the only one of the four that also keeps the training log and links the two.
