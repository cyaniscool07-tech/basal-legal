In short

Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is your basal metabolic rate multiplied by an activity factor from 1.2 to 1.9. The basal metabolic rate comes from Mifflin-St Jeor: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age, +5 for a man and −161 for a woman. The result is your maintenance calories, with a typical margin of error of about 10%.

This number is an estimate, not a measurement. It gives the average expenditure of people who resemble you on four variables. It ignores your muscle mass, your thyroid and your dieting history. Validation studies usually report around 10% error, which is 250 kcal a day on an expenditure of 2,500.

How to make it accurate

The only way to know your real expenditure is to observe it: compare what you eat with how your weight actually changes over several weeks. That is what Basal does, starting from this same calculation and correcting it as your data comes in.

The calculation in detail

Mifflin-St Jeor gives the basal metabolic rate: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age, then + 5 for a man and − 161 for a woman (weight in kg, height in cm). Daily expenditure is that result multiplied by the activity factor, from 1.2 to 1.9.

The activity factor is the weak link: between “sedentary” and “moderately active”, the gap is over 500 kcal a day. That is why the selector shows step equivalents: an observable reference is better than a feeling. They do not enter any calculation: they help you place yourself, not classify yourself.
