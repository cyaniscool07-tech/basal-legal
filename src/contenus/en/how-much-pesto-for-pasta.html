How much pesto per 100 g of pasta? The amounts, and their calories

Depending on the source, the advice ranges from 12 g to 50 g of pesto per 100 g of dry pasta: a fourfold difference. For an 80 g serving of pasta, that is anywhere from 37 to almost 200 kcal of sauce. The amount is not a detail: it decides whether the plate comes to 330 or 490 kcal.

In short

A French healthy-eating recipe card uses 12.5 g of pesto per 100 g of pasta; a pesto maker recommends 50 g. With a pesto at 370 kcal per 100 g (the Ciqual average), a tablespoon of about 15 g provides roughly 55 kcal. For 80 g of dry pasta (291 kcal), 20 g of pesto brings the plate to about 365 kcal.

How much pesto per 100 g of pasta

There is no official amount. Here is what three verifiable sources give, scaled to 100 g of dry pasta:

The manufacturer recommends four times more than the healthy-eating recipe card. A practical middle ground: one tablespoon (about 15 g) per 100 g of pasta already gives a clear colour and flavour, especially with a little cooking water to spread the sauce.

Pesto calories, amount by amount

For an 80 g serving of standard dry pasta (291 kcal according to Ciqual), with a pesto at 370 kcal per 100 g:

  • 10 g of pesto (MGP amount): 37 kcal, a plate of about 328 kcal;
  • 20 g (a slightly heaped tablespoon): 74 kcal, about 365 kcal;
  • 40 g (the manufacturer’s advice): 148 kcal, about 439 kcal.

With a richer pesto, such as Barilla’s (482 kcal per 100 g according to its label), the recommended 40 g come to 193 kcal and the plate to about 484 kcal. Almost all of these calories come from the oil: the Ciqual pesto sauce contains 35 g of fat per 100 g.

Homemade or jarred pesto

Homemade pesto is not necessarily lighter. The one in the Basal recipe (30 g of basil, 30 g of parmesan, 30 g of olive oil, 20 g of almonds and 5 g of garlic) comes to about 460 kcal per 100 g, based on the Ciqual values of each ingredient: the oil alone provides half. Its advantage lies elsewhere: you choose how much oil goes in. With 15 g of oil instead of 30, the same pesto loses about 135 kcal.

For a jar, the label is the reference: values vary widely from one brand to another.

Keeping the flavour with less pesto

  • Keep a ladle of cooking water: the starch binds the sauce, and a small amount of pesto coats all the pasta.
  • Mix off the heat, in the serving dish: the basil keeps its aroma.
  • Add volume that counts for little: cherry tomatoes, courgettes, rocket.
  • Add protein (chicken, tuna, chickpeas) rather than more pasta: the plate is more filling for the same calories.

What we don’t know

The “right” amount of pesto is a matter of taste: no source is authoritative, and the three cited here do not agree. The pesto calories given are those of the Ciqual average (370 kcal per 100 g) or of a specific label; your jar may differ. The weight of a tablespoon depends on whether it is level or heaped: weighing is safer.
