Pork tenderloin: protein and calories per 100 g, raw and cooked

Pork tenderloin provides 21 g of protein and 123 kcal per 100 g raw, 26 g and 168 kcal once cooked. It is the leanest cut of pork: closer to chicken than to a pork chop.

TS By Théo Serrano, creator of Basal · Published on 27 November 2026

You are cooking a pork tenderloin and want to know how much protein is in your portion. With Basal: you log the weight you measured, choosing the raw or cooked line; the values come from the Ciqual table of ANSES, the French food safety agency.

In short

100 g of raw pork tenderloin: 21.2 g of protein, 4.1 g of fat, 123 kcal. Cooked: 26.1 g of protein, 7.1 g of fat, 168 kcal. A 150 g raw serving provides about 32 g of protein for 185 kcal.

The cooked value shows more protein per 100 g because the meat has lost water: the protein is more concentrated, not more plentiful. Based on these values, and assuming the protein stays in the meat, 100 g of raw tenderloin gives about 81 g cooked. The guide weighing food raw or cooked explains which value to pick depending on when you weigh.

How much protein in a serving of pork tenderloin

  • 120 g raw: 25 g of protein, 148 kcal;
  • 150 g raw: 32 g of protein, 185 kcal;
  • 200 g raw: 42 g of protein, 246 kcal.

The weight of the whole tenderloin is on the pack: divided by the number of servings, it gives your raw portion. The pork tenderloin with mustard sauce recipe uses 400 g for 4 servings, about 28 g of protein per plate with the sides.

Pork tenderloin remains a lean meat, on a par with 5% beef mince. Turkey and chicken do a little better. The pork chop, more than twice as fatty, provides 30% less protein per 100 kcal. The different fat levels of beef mince are covered in beef mince calories.

What we don’t know

Ciqual values are averages: a poorly trimmed tenderloin, with its membrane and fat, has a few more calories. Water loss depends on cooking; the cooked weight worked out above (81 g per 100 g raw) is an estimate drawn from the table values, not a measurement. Cooking fat and sauce come on top and are not counted here.
