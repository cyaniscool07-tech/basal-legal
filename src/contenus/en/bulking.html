Losing or gaining weight

Bulking: calories, protein and pace

Building muscle takes a little more energy than you spend, enough protein and training that progresses. The hard part is not gaining weight: it is gaining it mostly as muscle.

How many extra calories?

Start from your daily expenditure (estimate it with the calculator) and add 5 to 10%, usually 150 to 300 kcal a day. A bigger surplus does not build more muscle: the body has a limited building speed, and the excess goes to fat.

Man, 30, 78 kg, moderately active (expenditure ≈ 2,710 kcal)2,850 to 2,980 kcal
Woman, 30, 62 kg, moderately active (expenditure ≈ 2,080 kcal)2,180 to 2,290 kcal

Lean or fast bulk?

A lean bulk aims for a moderate surplus, the one described above: you gain weight slowly, with little fat. A fast bulk pushes the surplus to 500 kcal or more: the scale climbs quickly, but a good part of it will have to be lost afterwards during a cut.

For most lifters, the lean version pays off better: less fat to lose afterwards, so more time spent building.

How fast to gain weight?

A common benchmark: 0.25 to 0.5% of your weight a week if you are a beginner (at 78 kg, about 0.2 to 0.4 kg), and rather half that if you have been training for several years, because gains slow down with experience.

As with weight loss, judge that pace on the two-to-three-week trend, not on a single weigh-in: water and carbs stored in muscle move weight from one day to the next.

How much protein?

Meta-analyses commonly put the useful intake between 1.6 and 2.2 g of protein per kilo of body weight per day. At 78 kg, that is about 125 to 170 g; at 62 kg, about 100 to 135 g. Beyond that, the benefit flattens. For your own number: work out your protein needs.

The rest of the calories is shared between carbs, which fuel training, and fat, best kept above roughly 20% of total calories. For meal ideas, Basal’s recipes show their protein per serving.

The surplus is useless without training that progresses. Load, reps or volume going up over the weeks is what gives the body a reason to build. For volume, see how many sets per muscle per week.

When to adjust?

  • Weight has not moved for three weeks: add 100 to 150 kcal.
  • Weight is rising clearly faster than planned and your waist follows: take off 100 to 150 kcal.
  • Loads stall while weight goes up: look at sleep and your programme first, not calories.

In practice

  • Add 5 to 10% to your expenditure, 150 to 300 kcal a day.
  • Aim for 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kilo, spread over the day.
  • Gain 0.25 to 0.5% of your weight a week, less if you are experienced.
  • Adjust in small steps based on the trend, never on a single weigh-in.
