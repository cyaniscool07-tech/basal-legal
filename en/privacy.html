Privacy policy — Basal

Last updated: 18 October 2026

This is a translation of the French privacy policy. If the two versions ever differ, the French version prevails.

This policy describes exactly what the Basal app does today, without anticipating features that are not yet available.

1. Summary

  • No account is required to use the app. Without an account, your personal data (profile, weight, meals, activity, goals — see section 2) stays entirely on your device and is sent to no one: it only leaves the device if you choose to export it yourself (section 8).
  • If — and only if — you create an account (see section 3), your data is backed up on a server located in Ireland, in the European Union, so that you can get it back on another device. You can delete this account at any time, which also erases this data from the server (section 10). Some information still stays specific to each device and is never sent — the list is in section 4.
  • No advertising tracker, no advertising, no profiling. Only anonymous usage counters (for example “the Premium screen was opened”, “a first meal was logged”) are sent, never any value you entered (section 6).
  • The Community (shared recipes, routines and programmes) can be browsed without an account. Publishing requires an account and a username: only that username and what you choose to publish become public — never your email address or your tracking (section 5a).
  • A technical diagnostic tool (section 6) receives crash reports and these anonymous usage counters. It never receives your personal data (weight, meals, goals…).

2. Data you record in the app

When you use Basal, you can record:

  • your profile (height, age, sex, activity level, goal, target weight);
  • your weight and its history;
  • the meals and foods you log (name, quantity, macronutrients);
  • your daily step count (entered manually, or synced automatically from Health Connect on Android or the Health app on iPhone, if you turn this option on — see section 9);
  • your workouts (category, intensity, duration — calories burned are calculated automatically by the app, never entered manually);
  • your calorie and nutrition goals, and the nutrition strategy you chose;
  • your reminder/notification preferences, your theme (light/dark) and your language;
  • your personal recipes, and what you organise in the Meals tab: meal plan, shopping list (including what you say you already have at home), collections and favourites, recurring meals, batches (portions, fridge or freezer) and cooking sessions;
  • your food preferences: allergens and foods you exclude, foods you dislike;
  • your progress and unlocked achievements, calculated from the data above.

This data is considered health and wellbeing data in the broad sense (weight, diet, physical activity, and any allergies you declare in your food preferences). It is handled with the same care as any sensitive data: it stays on your device and is shared with no one by the app itself.

Meal, batch-cooking and plan-adjustment suggestions (“Plan my meals”, “Prep my week”, “Optimise my week”) are calculated on your device, from the app’s recipes and your data. No data is sent to an artificial intelligence service or any third party to produce them.

An account remains entirely optional (see section 3): if you don't create one, the app asks for no email address, no username, no phone number and no password. Even if you create an account, none of this data is linked to your civil identity — it is only used to power the calculations and charts shown in the app.

3. Account (optional)

If you wish, you can create an account from Settings → Account — never forced on you when the app starts, never required to use it fully. An account is used to back up your data and get it back on another device, and to carry a Premium subscription from one platform to another.

Creating an account records:

  • your email address, required: it identifies your account and lets you sign back in;
  • a password, if you choose this sign-up method rather than Google or Apple (see below). The app never stores it as is: it is passed to our authentication provider, which only keeps an encrypted hash of it; neither the publisher nor the app can read it;
  • a display name, optional;
  • a technical identifier and the date the account was created.

Your address must be confirmed through a link sent by email before the account can be used. You can sign out, reset your password, or permanently delete your account at any time from the same screen.

Signing in with Google or Apple

Instead of choosing a password, you can create your account — or sign back in — through Google or Apple. The resulting account is exactly the same, with the same data and the same delete button.

In that case, your Google or Apple password is never passed to the app: the provider recognises you on its own side, then hands us a signed confirmation. From it we only take your email address and a technical identifier — never your contacts, calendar, photos or anything else from your Google or Apple account.

If you go through Apple, it offers to hide your address. If you accept, you give us a relay address ending in @privaterelay.appleid.com: it works normally to receive our messages, but does not reveal your real address. The app treats it like any other address.

Signing out also ends the session on Google's side, so that the previous account is not automatically selected again the next time you sign in.

Deleting the account erases your data from the server, but not from your device: weight, meals, recipes and goals remain there, and the app simply becomes local again, as it was before the account was created.

4. Where this data is stored

Without an account, all your data described in section 2 is stored only on your device, using the app's native storage. None of it is sent to a server: uninstalling the app deletes it permanently, and switching devices without using the manual export (section 8) transfers none of it.

With an account, the same data is also copied to a server hosted by Supabase, in the Ireland (European Union) region. There it is linked to your account and protected by isolation that technically prevents any other user from accessing it. This copy is used to give it back to you on another device.

Some information stays specific to each device and is never sent, because it would make no sense elsewhere or would disrupt another device:

  • the notification identifiers assigned by your phone;
  • the state of the Health sync (Health Connect on Android, Apple Health on iPhone), which is specific to the device;
  • your cached subscription status, which always comes from the App Store or the Play Store, never from us;
  • the technical identifier of the installation and the anonymous diagnostic identifier;
  • any strength workout currently in progress on this phone.

Finally, if an unexpected technical error occurs (bug, crash), an anonymous, purely technical report — never your personal data — may be sent to a third-party diagnostic service (see section 6).

5. Sharing data with third parties

The publisher of the app does not sell, rent or share any of your data with a third party, for advertising, commercial or any other purposes. No third-party audience analytics (behavioural analytics), advertising or ad tracking tool is built in.

The app makes one kind of network request triggered by you — food search and barcode scanning: when you type a search, the text you typed is sent to the public, collaborative Open Food Facts database; when you scan a barcode, only the barcode number is sent to it to identify the product.

No other information (your profile, your weight, the meals you have already logged) is sent on these occasions. These requests are subject to the privacy policy of Open Food Facts, a project independent from the publisher of this app.

A second kind of network request exists, triggered not by a deliberate action but by an unexpected technical error: see section 6 (technical diagnostics / Sentry).

Finally, if you have created an account, the app communicates with Supabase, our hosting and authentication provider, to back up your data there and check your identity when you sign in. Supabase acts solely on our behalf and on our instructions; it does not use your data for its own purposes. The servers used are located in Ireland, in the European Union. Without an account, no such communication takes place.

Account emails (confirming your address, forgotten password) are sent by Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, France), our email delivery provider. It only receives your email address and the content of that email, acts solely on our behalf and processes this data in the European Union. Links in these emails go through a Brevo click counter set to anonymous mode: a click is never linked to your address. No marketing email is ever sent.

5a. Community

The Community is a library of recipes, routines and programmes shared by people who use Basal, checked before they become visible. It is not a social network: no messaging, no followers, no detailed profile.

Browsing and importing do not require an account. The app then queries the server (Supabase, Ireland) with your search and filters (for example “vegetarian” or “30 min or less”); they are not stored, and nothing from your tracking is sent. Imported content becomes a personal recipe or routine on your device, as if you had created it. If you are signed in, the import is recorded (which content, which version, when); this information is never made public.

Publishing requires an account. The server then stores:

  • your username (3 to 24 characters), which you choose — never your email address or display name;
  • the version of the Community rules you accepted, and the date;
  • each publication and each of its versions, exactly what the preview shows before sending: for a recipe, its name, description, servings, times, steps and ingredients with their weights; for a routine, its exercises, sets, reps and rest times; for a programme, its days and routines;
  • the moderation decisions concerning it, with their reason;
  • your review requests.

What is never published: your email address, your log, your weight and weigh-ins, your loads, your workouts, your records, your private notes. A publication is built from a closed list of fields: anything not on it never leaves your device. Nutritional values are recalculated by the server from the ingredients.

What is public: your username and approved content, visible to anyone who uses Basal, with or without an account.

Reports and blocks. When you report content, the server keeps the reason, your optional comment and your account identifier; the author never knows who reported it. You can follow what happened to your reports in “My publications”. Blocking an author is recorded so that you no longer see their content; they are not notified.

Moderation. The publisher reviews publications and reports. Every decision (approval, rejection, hiding, suspension) is explained, communicated to the person concerned and recorded in a log that cannot be edited or erased: this is what makes it possible to justify a decision and handle a dispute.

Retention.

  • A publication stays visible until you withdraw it, or until moderation removes it.
  • Deleting your account immediately deletes your username, your publications and their versions, your blocks, your imports and your review requests.
  • Your reports are kept without any link to you, for the review in progress; the moderation log keeps its decisions anonymised (without your identifier).
  • Copies that other people have already imported belong to them: they are personal recipes and routines, on their devices.

Hosting: the same as for accounts — Supabase, in Ireland (European Union).

6. Technical diagnostics and anonymous usage counters

The app uses Sentry, a third-party technical diagnostic service, to be notified automatically when a bug or crash occurs so that it can be fixed. It is not an ad tracking tool: no profile is built, nothing is cross-referenced with other services.

The same tool also receives a few anonymous usage counters, to know whether the app does its job and to improve its flows: for example “onboarding was completed”, “an onboarding step was completed”, “a first meal was logged”, “the Premium screen was opened or closed”, “a purchase was started, completed or cancelled” (with the chosen plan: monthly or yearly), “reminders were accepted or postponed”. These counters say that an action happened, never its value: no weight, no calories, no food, no content you entered. They are linked to the same anonymous installation identifier as crash reports, never to your account.

What is sent to Sentry when an error occurs:

  • technical information about the error itself (error message, stack trace, screen or feature involved — e.g. "food search", "export", never the content you entered);
  • standard technical information about the device (app version, operating system, device model) — the same kind of information shown in the native iOS/Android crash reports;
  • an anonymous, randomly generated identifier created when the app is installed, separate from any account you may have, which cannot be linked to your identity or your email address — it is only used to link several reports from the same device, to check whether a fix actually solves the problem.

What is never sent to Sentry: your profile, your weight, your meals, your goals, your workouts, your account information, or any other data described in sections 2 and 3 — nor your IP address (the tool's default collection of personal data is explicitly turned off in its configuration). No photo or location data is collected.

Where and when:

  • This tool is turned off during development of the app; it is only active in distributed versions (beta and the version published on the stores).
  • The data is hosted by Sentry on servers located in the European Union (Germany).
  • Sentry acts as the publisher's technical processor within the meaning of the GDPR, for this diagnostic purpose only. Its own privacy policy applies to the hosting of these technical reports: see sentry.io/privacy.

7. Notifications

The app can send you local reminders (weigh-in, unlogged meal, daily summary) if you turn this feature on. These notifications are scheduled entirely on your device: no external server is involved, and no data is sent to generate these reminders.

8. Exporting your data

At any time, you can export all your data (profile, weight, meals, steps, workouts, metabolism, personal recipes, food preferences, meal plan, shopping list, recurring meals and batches) as JSON or CSV, from Settings → My data. This file is generated on your device and is:

  • either downloaded directly (web version);
  • or shared through your device's native share sheet (mobile), which lets you choose where to send it (email, personal cloud storage, etc.).

The publisher of the app has no access to any of these exports: the choice of destination is entirely yours.

9. Permissions requested by the app

The app requests up to three permissions on your device, each tied to a specific feature, never turned on by default without your consent, and which can be turned off at any time in your phone's settings:

  • Camera: used only to scan the barcode of a food product in order to pre-fill its nutrition information. No photo or video is taken, saved or sent — the camera is only used to read the barcode live, while the scan screen is open. The app does not request microphone access.
  • Notifications: used only for the local reminders you set up yourself (weigh-in, meals, end-of-day summary). These notifications are generated and scheduled entirely on your device; they do not go through any remote push notification service.
  • Health data (daily steps): only if you turn on syncing yourself from Settings → Health sync, the app reads your daily step count already recorded by Health Connect on Android or by the Health app on iPhone. Always read-only: it never writes anything to Health, and reads no other category — no heart rate, sleep, weight or workouts. This permission stays off by default and can be revoked at any time from your phone's settings. On iPhone, the value read is the aggregated total from the Health app: it may therefore include steps measured by an Apple Watch or by another app, if you use one.

You can refuse or revoke these permissions at any time; the app remains usable, and only the corresponding features (barcode scanning, reminders, step sync) will then be unavailable — entering steps manually always remains possible.

10. Deleting your data

You can delete your data at any time:

  • item by item, by deleting a weigh-in, a meal or a workout;
  • your account (see section 3), from Settings → Account, without deleting your local data;
  • entirely, by clearing the app's data from your device's system settings, or by uninstalling the app (all local data is then deleted by the operating system).

Without an account, no copy of your data exists outside your device: there is therefore nothing more to do with the publisher.

How long is your data kept?

For as long as you keep your account, and not a minute longer. There is no automatic expiry: someone who comes back after two years finds their history intact, which is precisely the point of long-term tracking. Your data is kept for as long as the service is provided, then deleted when you delete your account.

Without an account, the question does not arise: nothing is kept anywhere other than on your device, and uninstalling the app erases everything.

How to delete

With an account, deleting your account from Settings → Account also immediately erases all the associated data on the server. This deletion is permanent and requires no email request: it is available directly in the app, as the App Store and the Play Store require. Your local data stays on your device until you erase it yourself.

If you have uninstalled the app or changed phones, the steps to follow — along with the exact details of what is and isn't deleted — are on the account deletion page (in French).

11. Privacy of minors

This app is reserved for people aged 18 and over.

This threshold is stricter than the law requires, and that is deliberate. In France, the age of digital consent is 15: above it, a person can consent on their own to the processing of their data. Nothing therefore required excluding 15-to-17-year-olds.

Two reasons led us to do so anyway.

The first is the subject itself. Calorie tracking touches on sensitive issues, and eating disorders particularly affect teenagers. Counting calories every day is a behaviour that the scientific literature documents as a risk factor at that age. The app shows warnings to that effect, offers no aggressive goal and in no way replaces the advice of a healthcare professional — but no interface precaution makes up for the exposure itself.

The second is the data. A weight, daily weigh-ins and food intake are health data. For a minor, they fall under a stricter regime, and processing them requires parental consent that this app does not put in place — and therefore does not claim to cover.

No data is knowingly collected from anyone under 18. If you find that a minor has created an account, write to the address in section 14: the account and the associated data will be deleted.

This choice is carried over to the distribution platforms: the age rating is set to 18 and over, and access for users identified as minors is restricted. The app is not enrolled in any programme aimed at children.

12. Legal basis and your rights

On what basis is this data processed

  • Account, backup and return of your data — performance of a contract: it is the service you request by creating an account (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Managing a Premium subscription — performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Anonymous crash reports — legitimate interest: keeping the app in working order, without identifying anyone (Art. 6(1)(f)).
  • Community: username, publications, imports — performance of a contract: it is the service you request by publishing (Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Community: moderation, reports, blocks, decision log — legal obligation under the EU Digital Services Act (Art. 6(1)(c)), and legitimate interest: keeping the library safe and being able to justify every decision (Art. 6(1)(f)).

No processing is based on advertising, profiling or resale: there simply isn't any.

Your rights

Without an account, the publisher neither receives nor stores any of your personal data: the rights of access, rectification and erasure under the GDPR are exercised directly by you, on your device — you are the only person who holds this data (see sections 8 and 10).

With an account, a copy of your data is hosted in Ireland, in the European Union. Your rights are then exercised as follows:

  • access and portability: the export feature (section 8) produces all your data at any time in an open, readable format;
  • rectification: any data can be corrected directly in the app, the correction replacing the version stored on the server;
  • erasure: deleting your account from Settings → Account immediately and permanently erases the data from the server (section 10), with no request to make to the publisher;
  • objection: you can stop using the account at any time and go back to local mode, which remains fully functional.

For any question or complaint, the contact address is in section 14. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the CNIL, the French data protection authority.

The only exception, limited to the anonymous technical reports described in section 6: as they are attached to no identity (even if you have created an account, this anonymous identifier is never linked to it), they cannot be traced back to you specifically to exercise an individual right of access or erasure — they are kept by Sentry under its own retention policy, then deleted automatically.

13. Future changes

If an additional health sync (Garmin, Fitbit) or any other processing involving a new transfer of data were to be offered, this policy would be updated beforehand to describe it precisely, and the feature would be presented to you as optional — exactly as the account described in section 3 already is.
